Chapter toolkit

Find your elected officials, reach the right staff, host the conversation, and keep the relationship going after Legislative Awareness Week ends.

Personal capacity. You take part as a private citizen and AFSA member: out of uniform, on personal time, from your personal email. Introduce yourself as a constituent and AFSA member, not as a representative of your unit, the Air Force, or the Department of War.

Legislative Awareness Week

One week, every chapter, one message: the quality of life of the enlisted force and their families.

Before the week

  • Look up your members and note the district or state office phone number, not the Washington, D.C. office.
  • Check Congress.gov for AFSA-supported bills the Member sponsors or cosponsors, so you can open with a thank-you.
  • If you want a proclamation, submit the request 45–60 days ahead.
  • Personalize your scripts and templates below.
  • Pick the top priorities that fit your district and copy their talking points.

During the week

  • Meet the Member or district staff, in person or by phone, and walk them through this year’s top priorities.
  • Host a fireside chat, or invite the Member to bring remarks to a chapter meeting.
  • Share personal experiences that illustrate impacts to readiness and quality of life.
  • Leave a one-pager with concise, district-level perspectives.

Phone script

Ask for a meeting

Hello, my name is [Your Name], and I’m calling as your constituent and a member of the Air Force Sergeants Association. We are looking to set up a time for members of our local chapter to meet with the Congressman or staff regarding key policy issues impacting enlisted servicemembers, their families, and our veterans. Could you please provide the best contact information for the appropriate staff member so we can follow up to request a meeting? Thank you very much. We appreciate your time and support for our military community.

Proclamations and recognition

Proclamations raise community awareness and strengthen relationships with public officials.

How to request one

  1. 1

    Submit 45–60 days ahead

    Early requests are the easiest for a busy office to say yes to.

  2. 2

    Contact the local office

    Reach the district, constituent services, or scheduling office, not Washington, D.C., when applicable.

  3. 3

    Send the full package

    A draft proclamation, event details, and an AFSA point of contact.

  4. 4

    Follow up within a week

    A polite phone call keeps the request moving.

The request package

  • Cover email or letter
  • Draft proclamation
  • One-page event summary
  • AFSA organizational overview
  • Invitation to attend and present the proclamation

Who can issue recognitions

  • Governor
  • Lieutenant Governor
  • Mayor
  • County Judge or Commission
  • State Senator or Representative
  • U.S. Representative or Senator

Occasions worth recognizing

  • National POW/MIA Recognition Day
  • Veterans Day
  • Memorial Day
  • Military Appreciation Month
  • Air Force Birthday
  • Chapter anniversaries
  • Gold Star Family events
  • Community outreach events

After the meeting

Follow-up checklist

Engaging in your personal capacity

As a private citizen and AFSA member, you can

  • Meet with Members of Congress and staff, or host them at a chapter event
  • Share personal experiences that illustrate impacts to readiness and quality of life
  • Explain what pending legislation would do and support AFSA’s legislative priorities
  • Write personal letters to elected officials
  • Build professional relationships through education and dialogue

Every time, make sure you are

  • Out of uniform and on personal time
  • Using your personal email, never a government account
  • Introducing yourself as a constituent and AFSA member, without a duty title
  • Keeping AFSA’s priorities clearly separate from any official Department of War position

This is AFSA best-practice guidance, not legal advice. Consult your servicing Staff Judge Advocate or Ethics Counselor when questions arise.

  • DoDD 1344.10, Political Activities by Members of the Armed Forces
  • DoD 5500.07-R, Joint Ethics Regulation
  • DAFI 51-508, Political Activities, Free Speech and Freedom of Assembly
  • AFI 1-1, Air Force Standards

Talk about this year’s top priorities

The goal of every meeting is to walk the office through these priorities. Pick the ones that fit your district, copy the talking points, and add a personal experience that shows the impact. These are the bills AFSA carried to Capitol Hill at its March 2026 Hill Day; figures are as of that month.

For those currently serving

Pay Our Troops Act

Pay Our Troops Act, H.R. 5401; Senate counterpart, the Pay Our Military Act, S. 3002

  • Government shutdowns can delay pay for service members and their families.
  • Keeps military pay and allowances flowing during a lapse in appropriations, including for Department of War civilians and essential contractors who support military operations.
  • Pay disruptions affect quality of life, readiness, retention, and family financial stability.
  • AFSA’s first priority is full-year defense appropriations on time; this bill is a backstop.

IVF for Military Families Act

H.R. 2557 / S. 1231

  • Service-connected injuries and deployments can affect a service member’s ability to start a family.
  • Ensures TRICARE covers IVF treatment.
  • Closes the gap with federal civilian workforce health care in access, affordability, and quality.
  • Supporting family planning supports retention and readiness.

Military Family GI Bill Promise Act

H.R. 4540

  • Transferable education benefits give recruits a family-focused reason to serve.
  • Lets members who have earned the benefit pass it to their families, including after separation.
  • Members who have children later in life or after separation can still transfer their benefits.

Also targeted for advancement

  • Duty Status Reform Act, H.R. 6976
  • Military CARE Act, H.R. 6796
  • Health Care Fairness for Military Families Act, S. 2448 / H.R. 4768
  • Improving Access to Prenatal Care for Military Families Act, S. 2239, and its House companion, H.R. 4381

For veterans

Veteran mental health and suicide prevention

Written Informed Consent Act, S. 3314 / H.R. 4837; NOPAIN for Veterans Act, S. 3209 / H.R. 4509

  • An average of 17.6 veterans die by suicide each day.
  • 61% of VHA facilities face severe psychologist shortages, which lengthens wait times.
  • Require written informed consent before black box psychiatric medications are prescribed, so veterans understand the risks.
  • Expand the mental health workforce through competitive pay, loan forgiveness, and streamlined VA hiring.
  • Expand telehealth as a complement to in-person mental health care, not a replacement.

If you or a veteran you know is in crisis, call the Veterans Crisis Line: dial 988, then press 1.

Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy access

HBOT Access Act of 2025, S. 862

  • Traditional treatments show limited success for many veterans with PTSD and TBI.
  • Clinical studies show HBOT reduces PTSD symptoms and improves cognitive function in veterans with brain injuries.
  • Establish VA pilot programs to evaluate HBOT as a covered treatment option.
  • Increase research funding to validate HBOT as a standard, evidence-based VA treatment.

Major Richard Star Act

H.R. 2102 / S. 1032

  • More than 52,000 combat-injured veterans see their earned retirement pay reduced dollar for dollar by their VA disability compensation.
  • The bill allows concurrent receipt of retirement pay and VA disability compensation.
  • Retirement pay and disability compensation are different payments for different purposes.
  • 315 House and 77 Senate cosponsors, about three-quarters of Congress, as of March 2026.

Also targeted for advancement

  • Sharri Briley and Eric Edmundson Veterans Benefits Expansion Act, H.R. 6047
  • Guard and Reserve GI Bill Parity Act, H.R. 1423 / S. 649
  • Love Lives On Act, H.R. 1004 / S. 410
  • Caring for Survivors Act, H.R. 2055 / S. 611
  • Veteran Caregiver Reeducation, Reemployment & Retirement Act, H.R. 2148 / S. 879
  • Preserve VA disability compensation and military retirement pay in budget decisions
